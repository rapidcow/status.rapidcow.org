  Subject: rdkw.org Zone (first-time hostmaster?)

  So I wanted to set up a page for rdkw.org to show
  a domain broker I've been in touch with (basically
  to show him a forwarded version of the email I sent
  to the Kuwaiti registrant of rdkw.net whose domain
  I've been vying for ever since I... well, thought of
  it could be a viable substitute for yizx.net for my
  family), and Gmail "shockingly" butchers my RFC822
  attachment completely!

  Naturally I was going to change to an HTML part,
  until I remembered nobody actually opens an HTML
  file like a file.  And that's when I had the idea
  of putting it on my web server, and giving him a
  link to it.

  ... which led me to signing keys with dnssec-ksr,
  publishing the DS hash on my registrar (Porkbun), and
  replacing the nameservers with my BIND9 servers....
  (If you were wondering where that typo patch came from
  it was from me reading the DNSSEC tutorial on BIND9's
  ReadTheDocs... that's what I've been learning yeah :)

  It took a LOT of work (like, all the way from yesterday
  afternoon to midnight where I got tired, then 3 to 6am
  (when I woke up to see my roommate leave the balcony
  door open in the middle of a blizzard (granted I burnt
  my pizza leftover in the microwave causing an obscene
  amount of smoke in the living room but come on it's been
  hours since that happened!)), and 9am to now) but now
  having moved from Porkbun to my DNS servers I think it's
  working quite well!

  <https://dnsviz.net/d/git.rdkw.org/dnssec/>

  This Verisign validator also reminded me of how one
  (though it was the Ubuntu one, I'm not sure why?) of my
  servers wasn't reachable over IPv6 with UDP, so I also
  changed the YAML config in /etc/netplan on the Debian DO
  server by adding the IPv6 address and gateway...
  basically you can now reach it via IPv6.  (I haven't
  been able to test IPv6 much from my laptop, so sorry
  to anyone who tried to connect over IPv6!)

  And yeah... if you look at the SOA record it'd say
  something like:

  ; <<>> DiG 9.20.20 <<>> @1.1.1.1 +multi SOA rdkw.org
  ; (1 server found)
  ;; global options: +cmd
  ;; Got answer:
  ;; ->>HEADER<<- opcode: QUERY, status: NOERROR, id: 28639
  ;; flags: qr rd ra ad; QUERY: 1, ANSWER: 1, AUTHORITY: 0, ADDITIONAL: 1

  ;; OPT PSEUDOSECTION:
  ; EDNS: version: 0, flags:; udp: 1232
  ;; QUESTION SECTION:
  ;rdkw.org.		IN SOA

  ;; ANSWER SECTION:
  rdkw.org.		3600 IN	SOA ns1.rapidcow.net. hostmaster.rapidcow.org. (
  				1773771571 ; serial
  				43200      ; refresh (12 hours)
  				900        ; retry (15 minutes)
  				345600     ; expire (4 days)
  				7200       ; minimum (2 hours)
  				)

  (Note the "ad" (Authentic data) flag -- it means
  that Cloudflare approves of my DNSSEC signature! :)

  So I guess that makes me both the hostmaster and
  the webmaster now, eh?  To some extent, though
  I don't plan to host my own DNS forever... deSEC
  and Cloudflare do a good enough job.  The only
  thing I get annoyed at is that none of them let
  me upload my own zone file (well deSEC just
  straight up has no such interface (although it
  let me import the first time, I don't see any way
  to do it again?  (Might try reverse-engineering
  what it did the first time, come to think of it...
  but anything I do from here is extra work and will
  only delay me working on homework that I was
  supposed to get started on this Saturday so nyah!
  Not doing that! >n<)) and Cloudflare... basically
  does not replace the existing records and panics
  at the sight of any duplicate entries, so what I've
  been doing is delete everything; re-import it (and
  PRAY that Cloudflare hasn't propagated its deletion);
  then delete any extraneous records like NS records
  at the apex (because at the moment I don't want
  Cloudflare to list me as secondary zones; I don't
  have any DS records delegating to my own keys so
  the trust chain will break if my servers were to
  act as authoritative for those servers (which they
  are, but also they don't sign anything... again
  KSK rollover will be exhausting, and I don't really
  want to commit to it until I get a taste of how it
  feels like for another few years...)

  Either way, that's all you really have to know!
  (Oh and of course I made DNS over HTTPS, that's
  a necessity to me -- though if you were to use it
  your queries would not be anonymous to me (BIND9
  logs it), so just be careful with that... ;)
