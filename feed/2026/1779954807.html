  Date: Thu, 28 May 2026 02:53:27 -0500
  Subject: AI for code

  I have been using AI (Claude Code, namely) to write code,
  and while nobody has mentioned this point to me (yet), I
  ... still feel like I should say something.

  (Like doing a press release, ugh.... but if i don't say
  something, i can't sleep with this.)

  Basically, I don't think i can make a stance that i can
  stick to.  I am neither refraining from all AI-generated
  writing/code nor embracing it fully.  One thing I can
  take a strong stance against is the so-called AI-generated
  art; but that isn't to say I automatically like any art
  that isn't AI.  (It never has been the case, so why should
  it be now?)

  Another thing I can say is privacy: AI for my public code
  is alright, private code sometimes (it's not like I keep
  my diaries there), but data is where I draw the line.  I
  periodically clean up GPT and Claude's memory because I
  reveal too much, sometimes... it's mostly my real domain
  name I forget to deface, but I still prune them regularly
  to at least make an effort in obscuring my identity.

  A third line I can draw is quality: now that I am writing up
  a patch for Perl; you bet I will thoroughly compare Claude's
  analysis with real code and my rudimentary compiler knowledge.
  That also isn't worth a separate blog, I just realized...
  I'll let the GitHub bugtracking system speak for itself;
  anything I can't speak directly in front of the developers
  is probably not worth letting the rest of the world either.
  (Partially because I am still disgusted by that OCaml pull
  request; something like https://github.com/Perl/perl5/pull/24402
  in comparison at least feels more modest and accountable
  for when AI "goes wrong".)  But like in terms of copyright,
  yes, I will make sure it's not a blatant rip-off; and if
  I treat authorship more like "person to hold accountable"
  (rather than my usual Git metadata model which is to use
  the author identity as the physical machine used to change
  the files (and the committer as the machine used to commit
  it, if that's different)), then yes, I will put my name
  under it, not because I treat AI code as mine, but because
  AI cannot be held accountable, and people can't email
  <claude@anthropic.com> and ask about rationales for
  the change (not like it even "remembers" writing that.)

  It's probably just me but I hate it when I am told on the
  one hand to do it the hard way, and on the other succumb to
  AI.  Like computer science is pointless, and the job market
  likes AI-first candidate.  Point is I am already using AI,
  not because I am told one way or another; or that it means
  I take a stance for or against AI, about water usage, cooling,
  electricity whatnot, yada yada....  It just means I find it
  suitable for what I want: getting information fast.  Often
  weird niche information people don't ask about, like...
  running Nginx to proxy DoH from BIND 9 for my nameservers.
  (Like BIND 9 has zero concepts of virtual hosts; I had to
  use two loopback addresses and enforce ACL on that); or
  my super niche Git server that needs to have directories
  and fine-grained ACLs on ref namespaces, and audit logs,
  that I can absolutely ask no one about because nobody is
  this insane to track everything in a Git repository!  And
  what made you think I am a developer or in all this thing
  because i care about the industry?  computer isn't what i
  love; art is!  And I also like the way programming lets me
  express what I want.  It's one of those things that, once
  you discover software is made by people, you can't stop
  thinking about how they could have been made differently...
  but that's that!  I want to make things WORK and not suck,
  and -- if I am in the mood to -- explain how those work.
  And if AI helps in the process of making it work, I'll
  accept it.  And in return, if AI inspired an idea, I'll
  put a short "thanks" below it.  If AI generated the code
  entirely, I'd let the AI proudly put the model name in
  the author header than hide it in a Co-Authored-By field
  or any non-machine-parseable format.  But I won't look at
  the AI-generated code, run around and panic "what will
  people THINK about ME?!" and decide to start over again --
  for what?  Just because I can't be seen walking around
  with less than 100% of original thoughts?  In my OWN code?

  Of course, I won't get to do this when it's someone else's
  project, and the stakes are high; and rightfully so...
  This is what I will have to do for Perl 5.  I will also not
  do it when it's clear that this is copyrighted code... (but
  I would argue that's what git log is for; you would already
  be able to tell, most of the time, if Claude generated the
  code or i did.  (There is just a tiny exception where I
  copied the code straight from Claude Code in commit 3625a59;
  but even then, it is somewhat based on my past projects so
  *technically*, it's like generated on the basis of my own
  code... which I DID write!  Morally speaking it wasn't that
  much worse than collocating information (and frankly if we
  spend here all day analyzing on a per-case basis whether
  something counts as potential copyright infringement....
  Well. :x))

  You can see how this is exhausting right?  I try my best to
  be transparent about this, but at the end of the day I just
  want it to work.  And by the time I am done with it, i can
  get back to my websites, or whatever other archive / homenet
  project I have going on beyond your sights on the Internet...

  (Also please don't post this on HN if anyone even reads this
  ... I'm only doing this to alleviate my guilt.  I DON'T need
  more judgment than I already do judging myself and analyzing
  the ways this could go wrong and how I will deal with it.
  Writing code and wanting to share it with people shouldn't
  demand a whole lawyer's worth of knowledge... and I should
  be allowed to have a life outside of all this while having
  a voice of sorts to change things in a project that I would
  otherwise have not deserved to make changes in... and still
  not being the best of best at it.  I try to understand and
  follow all the guidelines and feedback, and not waste anyone's
  time; in return I just want peace when it's my own code....)
